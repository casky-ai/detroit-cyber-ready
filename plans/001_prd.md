# Detroit Cyber Ready: Product Requirements

**Know when your city is at risk, before an incident becomes an outage.**

Venture 313 AI Buildathon, September 2026. Live at **https://detroit-cyber-ready-web.vercel.app**.

- **Primary pillar:** **Open, Accessible & Responsible Government** (Rise Higher survey: *Open and Accessible Government*).
- **Secondary pillar:** **Safe, Just & Thriving Neighborhoods** (Rise Higher survey: *Safe and Just Communities*).

*Status: updated for submission on September 19, 2026. This document describes the product as built. Where the build differs from the original plan, the difference is stated in place and collected under [Changes from the first draft](#changes-from-the-first-draft).*

---

## Context

A sitting municipal CISO described the problem this way:

> "I have Tenable and other tools. I don't care about vulnerabilities. I want to be alerted if something is proactive."

That sentence rules out an entire product category. A continuous vulnerability assessment dashboard is something a city of Detroit's size already owns, often more than once.

What it does not own is the layer above. That layer:

- watches the outside world;
- notices when a change out there makes an existing exposure suddenly urgent;
- investigates it without a human;
- says which city service is at risk and what to do in the next hour.

Detroit Cyber Ready is not a scanner. It is a **signal to action pipeline**:

```
DETECT            INVESTIGATE                ACT
Something         Does it matter to          What should we fix
changed.          Detroit, and to which      right now, and who
                  city service?              owns it?
```

Positioning:

> Tenable tells Detroit what is vulnerable. Detroit Cyber Ready tells Detroit when something changes that could matter right now, investigates it automatically, determines which city service could be affected, and tells the team what to do next.

### Why these pillars

**Primary, Open, Accessible & Responsible Government.** A city is responsible for keeping the digital services residents depend on available and trustworthy. That responsibility does not stop at the services a resident touches directly. It extends to the shared infrastructure underneath them, which is where availability is actually lost.

**Secondary, Safe, Just & Thriving Neighborhoods.** The product is anchored on 911 Emergency Communications. Emergency response reaching people is the most direct link between City systems and neighborhood safety.

### Alignment to the Rise Higher Detroit survey

[Rise Higher Detroit](https://www.risehigherdetroit.com/rise-higher-detroit) is the City's process for turning community priorities into policy. More than 9,000 residents answered 19 questions. Two questions sit inside our primary pillar. The README maps both, answer by answer, with the City's charts:

- **Access to City Services** (18,694 responses from 7,495 respondents). Direct: *Online and Phone Tools* (28.3%). Enabling:
  - *In-Person Support in Neighborhoods* (45.6%)
  - *Improved Customer Service* (39.7%)
  - *Communication about Resources* (35.4%)
- **Trust in City Government** (17,973 responses from 7,495 respondents). Direct: *Provide Regular Reports* (29.2%). Enabling: *Develop a 311 Hotline* (35.3%).

Answers the product does not serve are listed there as not claimed. The underlying argument: every one of those answers assumes the City's systems are up and secure when a resident reaches for them. This product is the part of that promise that happens before the outage.

### Language discipline

External threat intelligence cannot prove a city is under attack. This product never says "you are being hacked" or "getting hacked in real time." It says "new external threat signal," "potential exposure," "evidence of targeting," and "requires investigation."

This is not hedging. Overstating an input is how a security product loses the trust of the person reading it, and a CISO who catches one overclaim stops believing the rest of the screen.

## 911 is the anchor

The most important thing a municipal CISO protects is 911. The product is built outward from that.

**911 is not a service in a list. It is the thing the list is measured against.**

Three consequences run through every section below.

### 1. A life-safety criticality tier

Above `critical`. Any confirmed exposure reaching a life-safety service is P1 regardless of what the numeric score says. A scoring formula that can talk a CISO out of paging someone about dispatch is a formula that eventually will.

### 2. 911 stays one box

The screen never breaks 911 down into CAD, PSAP, ESInet, or ANI/ALI. The moment the interface shows a seven-box breakdown of 911, the viewer is reading an architecture diagram instead of seeing a city service change state. The internal detail exists in the model's design notes. It does not render.

### 3. A dependency graph at exactly one level

City service depends on shared infrastructure. 911 depends on Remote Access, Identity, the Network Core, and (softly) Records Management. That is the entire depth of the graph.

This is the differentiated part of the product. The realistic path to a dispatch outage is not a vulnerability in a dispatch product. It is a vulnerability in a boring shared component the city runs for everything:

> Tenable can tell Detroit that a remote access appliance is vulnerable. It cannot tell Detroit that the appliance is one hop from 911.

**Resident impact is expressed in the only unit that matters here:** dispatch delay and response time. Not CVSS, not a risk score.

## Architecture

```
  THREAT SIGNALS (real, live)            SURFACE SIGNALS (synthetic snapshots)
  CISA KEV, NVD, EPSS, abuse.ch          seeded surface observations,
  polled every 6 hours                   diffed T0 -> T1
                                         (scanner output shape, no scanning)
            |                                      |
            +------------------+-------------------+
                               |
                               v
  packages/signals      normalize -> fingerprint -> dedupe -> provenance stamp
                               |
                               v
  packages/impact       STAGE 1: ASSET MATCH (deterministic, no LLM)
                        signal vendor/product/CPE  x  technology inventory
                               |
                        match? --no--> record "no Detroit exposure", show it, stop
                               |
                              yes
                               v
  packages/impact       STAGE 2: DEPENDENCY PROPAGATION
                        one hop, service <- shared infrastructure
                        "this VPN box is 1 hop from 911"
                               |
                               v
  packages/impact       RISK SCORE (explainable, 4 components)
                        + life-safety escalation rule
                               |
                               v
  packages/investigate  one agent per affected service, in parallel:
                        context (skills corpus + Casky platform, allSettled)
                        -> narrative -> action plan with owners and deadlines
                               |
                               v
  apps/web              six-step dashboard, streamed live over SSE
                        -> impact analysis -> action plan
                        -> incident ticket -> CISO alert in Slack

  FUTURE CONNECTORS (see docs/connectors.md)
  Tenable, SIEM, EDR, CMDB, Identity, Firewall  -->  feed the inventory
```

**The design principle that makes this defensible: the match is deterministic, only the narrative is generated.** A CISO can audit exactly why a signal fired, because the answer is a database row. The model never decides whether Detroit is exposed. It explains, prioritizes and writes, which is what it is good at.

## Upstream dependencies

Every line of Detroit-specific code here was written during the buildathon. The following are consumed as pinned upstream dependencies.

### The skills corpus

`upstream/skills` is a git submodule pinned to [mukul975/Anthropic-Cybersecurity-Skills](https://github.com/mukul975/Anthropic-Cybersecurity-Skills): 818 skills carrying `subdomain`, `tags`, `mitre_attack` and `nist_csf` frontmatter.

- We read `index.json` off disk.
- We narrow it to the skills relevant to the affected infrastructure before passing them to the agent.
- It is offline safe: no API call at request time.

### The Casky platform API

Consumed over HTTPS with an API key, scope `plans:read`.

| Endpoint | Use |
|---|---|
| `GET /api/v1/playbooks?techniques=T1190,T1133` | Matching investigation playbooks |
| `GET /api/v1/cve-spotlights?cve_ids=CVE-...` | Existing CVE analysis, CVSS, KEV flag, technique mapping |

Both sit behind `packages/investigate/src/casky.ts`, so an upstream outage degrades to an evidence gap rather than failing the investigation.

### OpenStreetMap

Street, park, and river geometry for the city map. It is downloaded once at build time from the Overpass API by `scripts/build-detroit-basemap.ts`, and it is licensed ODbL. The app shows attribution on the map.

### The investigation agent is written here

Not imported. The reason is a genuine design difference rather than convenience.

A general evidence classifier takes free-text input, a wall of logs someone pasted, and its first job is to work out what happened. Detroit Cyber Ready never has that problem. The input is already structured before the agent is invoked:

```ts
{ signal, landedOn, service, dependency, riskScore, riskComponents }
```

The deterministic layer has already answered two questions before the agent runs:

- **What happened:** we know the CVE, the asset it landed on, the service it reaches, and the dependency it reaches it over.
- **Does it matter:** the score and the escalation rule have already been applied.

The agent's job is narrower:

- explain the exposure in the city's terms;
- select investigation steps from the skills corpus;
- write an ordered action plan with owners and deadlines.

Architectural patterns carried over from prior work on Casky:

- `AbortController` passed in the options argument, never in params.
- A completion marker as the truth signal, checked independently at every layer.
- `Promise.allSettled` adapter fan-out where a failing source becomes a gap, never an exception.
- Prompt caching on stable system prompts.
- JSON repair on model output, because model JSON is unreliable by default.

## Data model

The source of truth is `supabase/migrations/0001_init.sql`. The shape, abridged:

```sql
create table city_services (
  slug              text primary key,       -- '911-emergency-communications'
  name              text not null,
  department        text not null,
  -- 'life-safety' is its own tier above 'critical'. 911 is the only member.
  criticality       text not null check (criticality in ('life-safety','critical','high','moderate','low')),
  resident_impact   text not null,          -- plain English, shown verbatim
  impact_unit       text,                   -- 'dispatch delay and response time'
  externally_exposed boolean not null default false,
  lat               numeric, lon numeric
);

-- The connective tissue. Shared across many services, NOT drawn on the map.
-- Deliberately small: four entries. This is the whole second tier.
create table shared_infrastructure (
  slug        text primary key,             -- 'remote-access' | 'identity' | 'network-core'
                                            -- | 'records-management'
  name        text not null,
  description text not null,
  exposure    text not null check (exposure in ('internet-facing','partner-network','internal'))
);

-- Exactly one level: a city service depends on shared infrastructure.
-- No infrastructure-to-infrastructure edges. No service-to-service edges.
create table service_dependencies (
  service_slug        text not null references city_services(slug),
  infrastructure_slug text not null references shared_infrastructure(slug),
  kind                text not null,        -- 'reachable-via' | 'authenticates-via'
                                            -- | 'routes-over' | 'reads-from'
  criticality         text not null check (criticality in ('hard','soft')),
  rationale           text not null,        -- one sentence, shown verbatim
  unique (service_slug, infrastructure_slug)
);

-- Technology attaches to a service OR to shared infrastructure, never both.
create table technologies (
  service_slug        text references city_services(slug),
  infrastructure_slug text references shared_infrastructure(slug),
  check (num_nonnulls(service_slug, infrastructure_slug) = 1),
  vendor text not null, product text not null, version text, cpe text,
  exposure text not null check (exposure in ('internet-facing','partner-network','internal'))
);

create table signals (
  id           uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  source       text not null,
  provenance   text not null check (provenance in ('live','synthetic')),
  external_id  text not null,               -- 'CVE-2023-46805'
  fingerprint  text not null unique,        -- sha256(source|external_id|material_fields)
  kind         text not null,
  title        text not null,
  published_at timestamptz not null,
  raw          jsonb not null,              -- the source record, e.g. KEV knownRansomwareCampaignUse
  first_seen_at timestamptz not null default now()
);

create table signal_matches (
  signal_id      uuid not null references signals(id) on delete cascade,
  landed_on      text not null,             -- slug of a service OR of infrastructure
  landed_kind    text not null check (landed_kind in ('service','infrastructure')),
  service_slug   text not null references city_services(slug),
  match_basis    text not null,             -- 'cpe' | 'vendor+product' | 'advisory-keyword' | 'surface-host'
  matched_on     jsonb not null,            -- exactly what matched: the audit trail
  hops           int not null default 0,    -- 0 direct, 1 via shared infrastructure; there is no 2
  dependency     jsonb,
  confidence     numeric not null
);

create table investigations (
  id               uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  signal_id        uuid not null references signals(id),
  service_slug     text not null references city_services(slug),
  status           text not null default 'queued'
                   check (status in ('queued','running','completed','failed','stopped')),
  context          jsonb,                   -- the match, dependency, and hop count
  risk_score       numeric(5,2),
  risk_components  jsonb,                   -- the four components, for the UI bars
  priority         text check (priority in ('P1','P2','P3','informational')),
  escalated        boolean not null default false,
  escalation_reason text,
  output           text,                    -- carries COMPLETE_MARKER on success
  started_at       timestamptz,
  ended_at         timestamptz,
  created_at       timestamptz not null default now()
);

create table actions (
  investigation_id uuid not null references investigations(id) on delete cascade,
  rank   int not null,
  title  text not null,
  detail text not null,
  sla    text not null,                     -- 'Now' | '< 1 hour' | '< 24 hours' | 'Ongoing'
  owner  text not null
);
```

`signal_matches.matched_on` is the audit trail. When a CISO asks "why did you flag 911," the answer is a row, not a model output.

## Signal sources

One interface, `SignalSource`, and every adapter implements it and passes the same contract suite.

| Adapter | Source | Key | Status | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `cisa-kev` | CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities feed | none | **Live, polled** | **Primary trigger.** Its `vendorProject` and `product` fields make a deterministic match possible. |
| `nvd` | NVD CVE API 2.0 | optional | **Live, polled** | CVSS, CWE, CPE configurations. CPE is the higher confidence match path. |
| `epss` | FIRST.org EPSS | none | **Live, per KEV CVE** | Exploitation probability, feeds the risk score. |
| `abusech` | URLhaus / ThreatFox | free | **Live, polled** | Malicious infrastructure. Corroborating signal only. |
| `greynoise` | Community API | free | Built, not yet wired into the poll | Mass scanning versus targeted. |
| `cisa-advisory` | CISA advisories | none | **Not built** | Planned campaign context. |

`/api/signals/poll` runs on a Vercel cron every 6 hours.

Shodan and Censys are **not called**. Their capability is replaced by a synthetic `SurfaceSource` emitting the same record shape, so the diff engine built here is the one that ships. See `docs/connectors.md`.

### Reliability rules

- Every fetch has a timeout (`AbortSignal.timeout`), 15s for NVD and 8s elsewhere.
- Every fetch is individually caught. One dead feed is an evidence gap, never a failed poll.
- Every signal is fingerprinted and upserted, so re-polling is idempotent.

**Not built: an offline snapshot fallback** (the planned `DEMO_MODE`). The stage demo does not need it: `/api/demo/start` injects one fixed, real KEV entry (CVE-2023-46805), so the demo never depends on the live feed being reachable. The investigations it starts still call the live model.

## Synthetic data strategy

> **Threat intelligence is real. The street map and the service addresses are real. Only the technology inventory and the dependency graph are simulated.** Every simulated source implements the exact interface its real replacement will implement, and both pass the same contract test suite.

This is the product posture, not a shortcut. Detroit Cyber Ready does not scan cities. A city supplies its inventory, or we ingest it from the tooling it already pays for. The simulated inventory stands in for a customer-supplied one, which is what the real thing will always be.

| Layer | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Threat intelligence | CISA KEV, NVD, FIRST EPSS, abuse.ch | **Real, live** |
| Street map: roads, parks, Detroit River | OpenStreetMap, built into the app | **Real** |
| Service addresses | Public City facility addresses, geocoded with OpenStreetMap Nominatim | **Real** |
| Which vendor and product each service runs | authored | **Simulated, labeled** |
| Dependencies between services and infrastructure | authored | **Simulated, labeled** |
| External attack surface observations | synthetic source | **Simulated, labeled** |

The service addresses are the buildings that house each department. They are not a claim about where any server sits.

### The map

The original design used MapLibre with third-party map tiles. Two tile providers (CARTO, then OpenFreeMap) both passed a server-side check but rendered blank in a presenter's browser, which blocked third-party map CDNs. A demo cannot depend on a viewer's network, so the map is now built at build time:

- **Built once, served locally.** `scripts/build-detroit-basemap.ts` downloads streets, parks, and water from OpenStreetMap once. It writes `apps/web/public/maps/detroit-basemap.svg` (about 206 KB), which is served from this app's own origin. There is no external request at runtime.
- **One projection.** `lib/map-projection.ts` is shared by the build script and the service pins, so pins always sit on the right streets. It corrects longitude for latitude, so the street grid stays square.
- **Focus + context lens.** A lens centred on downtown gives the dense cluster room: eight of the twelve services sit within about a kilometre of each other, and at true scale their pins would overlap. This is the transit-map technique. Streets stay continuous and pins stay on their real addresses relative to the streets.

### Seed data safety

Every hostname in seed data uses an RFC 2606 reserved domain (`.example`) and every address is inside an RFC 5737 documentation range. **Nothing in this repository points at a real system**, and `packages/signals/__tests__/seed-data-safety.test.ts` enforces it.

### Provenance

Every signal, observation and match carries `provenance`. The UI labels simulated inputs on every screen, and the CISO alert footer states what was live and what was simulated. This is also how the real product works: a city onboarding in week one has live threat feeds and a partially populated inventory, so mixed mode is the steady state.

## The exposure match

Deterministic, no LLM, two stages.

```ts
// packages/impact/src/exposure.ts

/** Stage 1: what did this signal land on, a service or shared infrastructure? */
export function matchSignalToAssets(signal: RawSignal, inventory: Technology[]): AssetMatch[]

/** Stage 2: if it landed on infrastructure, which city services depend on it? */
export function propagateToServices(matches: AssetMatch[], deps: ServiceDependency[]): SignalMatch[]
```

### Stage 1: asset match

1. **CPE match** (confidence 0.95). NVD `configurations` CPE against `technologies.cpe`, version range aware.
2. **Vendor plus product** (0.80). KEV `vendorProject` and `product` normalized (lowercase, strip punctuation and corporate suffixes).
3. **Surface host** (0.85). A `surface-change` signal carries a host that maps to a service or infrastructure entry.
4. **Advisory keyword** (0.45). Low confidence in the UI, never alone sufficient to raise a P1.

### Stage 2: dependency propagation

**One hop. Not a graph walk, a single join.**

- If the signal landed on a city service's own technology, `hops = 0`.
- If it landed on shared infrastructure, every service depending on that infrastructure is affected at `hops = 1`, with confidence multiplied by 0.85.

There is deliberately no max-hops constant, no breadth-first search, and no transitive closure. A second hop would be harder to defend to a CISO and impossible to draw legibly.

Worked example:

```
CISA KEV addition: remote access appliance, actively exploited
  -> stage 1: lands on infrastructure 'remote-access'   (vendor+product, 0.80)
  -> stage 2: 911-emergency-communications --[reachable-via, hard]--> remote-access
             1 hop, confidence 0.80 * 0.85 = 0.68
  -> service is life-safety tier, dependency is hard
  -> P1 escalation rule fires
```

Rendered as two boxes and one line, with the authored rationale verbatim:

> **Remote Access** ──hard──> **911 Emergency Communications**
>
> Dispatch reaches its systems through the remote access tier. If remote access is unavailable or compromised, dispatch is affected.

Stage 2 also finds the other services depending on that infrastructure: in the demo, Police, Water, Payments, and Courts. Each gets its own investigation. The blast radius panel names them; it draws only the one line.

### Non-matches are shown

No match means the signal is recorded as `no Detroit exposure` and displayed in the feed as informational. The threat feed has a filter for it. **Showing the signals that did not match is important.** It proves the system is filtering rather than alarming, which is the exact complaint the CISO had about existing tools.

## Risk scoring

Explainable, four components, rendered as four bars so a human can audit it.

```
risk = 40 * kev_active            // 1 if in CISA KEV, else 0
     + 25 * epss_percentile       // 0 to 1, from FIRST.org
     + 20 * criticality_weight    // life-safety 1.0, critical 0.8, high 0.6,
                                  //   moderate 0.4, low 0.2
     + 15 * reach_confidence      // stage 2 output, after hop decay

// reference example, pinned by a test:
// KEV on remote access, EPSS 0.85, reaching 911 (life-safety) at 1 hop, confidence 0.68
// 40*1 + 25*0.85 + 20*1.0 + 15*0.68 = 91.45  -> 91 / 100
//
// the live demo, with the real captured EPSS of 0.99983 for CVE-2023-46805:
// 40*1 + 25*0.99983 + 20*1.0 + 15*0.68 = 95.2  -> 95 / 100
```

Priority: `>= 80` P1, `60 to 79` P2, `40 to 59` P3, `< 40` informational.

In the demo, all five affected services score P1:

| Service | Score |
|---|---|
| 911 | 95 |
| Police | 91 |
| Water | 91 |
| Payments | 87 |
| Courts | 87 |

This is what the formula says for an actively exploited, near-certain-to-be-exploited CVE. See [Known limitations](#known-limitations-and-next-steps).

### The life-safety escalation rule

The formula can be overridden upward, never downward.

```ts
if (service.criticality === 'life-safety' && signal.kev_active && dependency.criticality === 'hard') {
  priority = 'P1';
  escalation_reason =
    'Life-safety service reachable over a hard dependency from actively exploited shared infrastructure.';
}
```

Shown in the UI and in the CISO alert whenever it fires, with its reason, so the override is visible rather than mysterious. Hardcoding a stated policy is correct behavior here.

### Resident impact is authored, not computed

It lives in `services.yaml` and is shown verbatim, because a sentence a human wrote is more trustworthy and more quotable than one a model wrote.

```yaml
- slug: 911-emergency-communications
  name: 911 Emergency Communications
  department: Detroit Public Safety / ITS
  address: Detroit Public Safety Headquarters, 1301 3rd Street, Detroit, MI 48226
  criticality: life-safety
  impact_unit: dispatch delay and response time
  resident_impact: >
    Potential disruption to emergency call handling, dispatch, and response times.
```

Dependencies live in `dependencies.yaml`, one rationale per edge, written to be read aloud. 911's edges:

- **Hard:** remote access, identity, and the network core.
- **Soft:** records management. Loss degrades context; it does not stop dispatch.

**The soft edge is deliberate:** it gives the system something to correctly not escalate, which is how it proves it filters rather than alarms.

   - tabs for city map, services, threat intel, alerts, and reports;
   - the street map with 12 service pins;
   - live counts of operational, at-risk, and critical services.

   Selecting a pin opens a callout on the map with status, address, resident impact, and a link to its investigation.
2. **New threat intel detected.** A real CISA KEV entry, CVE-2023-46805, an Ivanti Connect Secure authentication bypass, with related intelligence. Each item is labeled live data or simulated.
3. **Casky agents start investigating.** `/api/demo/start` injects the signal and creates one real investigation per affected service. The page opens one SSE stream per investigation. The 911 agent's six steps check off with real timestamps, and all five agents report in parallel. In live runs all five complete in under a minute; observed runs finish in roughly 10 to 15 seconds.
4. **Risk identified.** The same dashboard, with the affected pins turned critical, a callout on 911, and updated counts.
5. **Impact analysis.** For 911, with tabs:
   - **Overview:** the score, the escalation reason, the key findings, and the resident impact.
   - **Evidence:** the exposure path, and the agent's written analysis.
   - **Risk analysis:** the four scoring bars.
   - **Remediation.**
   - **Timeline.**
6. **Action plan delivered.** The agent's ranked actions with deadlines and owners, an incident ticket, and a CISO alert sent to Slack.

The step list on the page is typed against the agent's own step names (`InvestigationStepName`), so the page cannot show a step the agent never emits.

### The CISO alert

The alert is for a reader who wants only alerts they can act on. `/api/alerts/slack` receives investigation ids only. It reads everything else from the database and formats it with a pure function (`lib/slack-alert.ts`), so the message cannot say anything the records do not. In order:

- **Headline:** names the service and priority ("P1: 911 Emergency Communications exposed"), not the CVE number.
- **Bottom line:** what is exploited, why it reaches Detroit, and what to do now.
- **Key facts:** priority and escalation, risk score, exposure path, signal, resident impact, and owning department.
- **Action plan:** the full ranked plan for the highest-risk service, with deadline, detail, and owner.
- **Key findings:** deterministic, each traceable to the match or the score.
- **Other affected services:** listed once, with how each depends on the shared tier. The shared plan is not repeated.
- **Timeline:** from stored timestamps, labeled by what happened.
- **Buttons:** investigation, incident ticket, dashboard.
- **Footer:** states what was live and what was simulated.

### Resetting between runs

Every run leaves its investigations on the board. Before a presentation:

- open `/reset`;
- or use **Reset demo** on the dashboard (shown inline at step 1 whenever earlier results exist);
- or run `pnpm demo:reset`.

It clears investigations and their actions; signals and the inventory are kept. `/api/demo/reset` only accepts `POST` requests whose `Origin` matches the site. Link previews, crawlers, and other sites cannot trigger it.

## Completion guards

An investigation must reach a terminal state through at least three independent paths. A demo that hangs is a lost demo, and a production alert that silently never arrives is worse.

| Layer | Responsibility | Guard |
|---|---|---|
| L1 Anthropic API | Token generation and stream termination | `AbortController` hard wall, signal in the **options** argument, not params |
| L2 Investigation writer | Content assembly and atomic status write | Single `UPDATE` carrying `status`, `output` with `COMPLETE_MARKER`, and `ended_at` |
| L3 Heal cron | Rescue orphaned investigations, every 5 minutes | Detect the marker before ever writing `failed` |
| L4 SSE route | Stream to the client | Poll for the marker, not just the `status` column |
| L5 UI | Last resort escape hatch | A stale banner below the SSE timeout |

`COMPLETE_MARKER` lives in `packages/investigate/src/constants.ts`, is re-exported by `apps/web/lib/constants.ts`, and is never hardcoded.

**SSE contract:**

- 2000ms poll and 270000ms maximum duration, under the Vercel function limit.
- Unnamed `data` frames for output deltas.
- Named `step`, `done`, and `timeout` events.
- A heal step that completes a run whose marker landed but whose status write was lost.

## Known limitations and next steps

- **Every affected service scores P1 in the demo.** With an actively exploited CVE at the 99.98th EPSS percentile, the KEV and EPSS components alone contribute about 65 points. That puts even services with only a soft dependency (Water, Payments, Courts) over the P1 line. The life-safety rule only ever raises priority; a counterpart that caps soft-dependency reach below P1 is the next scoring change to consider.
- **Two planned feeds are not live.** GreyNoise is built but not wired into the poll. CISA advisories were planned and not built.
- **No offline mode.** The stage demo uses a fixed real KEV entry, but the investigations still call the live model. A snapshot fallback remains a next step.
- **The inventory is simulated.** In production a city supplies it or we ingest it from Tenable, a CMDB, and endpoint tooling. The connector contracts for that exist (`docs/connectors.md`); the connectors do not.
- **The reset endpoint is open to anyone using the site.** It is guarded by method and origin, not by authentication. That is right for a demo; a production build would put it behind sign-in.
- **Market figures are not stated.** Municipality counts and grant program figures must be verified before they appear on a slide or in this document.
