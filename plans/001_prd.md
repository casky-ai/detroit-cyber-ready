## Context

The City of Detroit CISO said, in person: "I have Tenable and other tools. I don't care about vulnerabilities. I want to be alerted if something is proactive."

That sentence kills the original product idea. A continuous vulnerability assessment dashboard is a tool he already owns twice over. What he does not own is the layer above it: something that watches the outside world, notices when a change out there makes one of his existing exposures suddenly urgent, investigates it without a human, and tells him which city service is at risk and what to do in the next hour.

So Detroit Cyber Ready is not a scanner. It is a **signal to action pipeline**:

```
DETECT            INVESTIGATE                ACT
Something         Does it matter to          What should we fix
changed.          Detroit, and to which      right now, and who
                  city service?              owns it?
```

Positioning line for the pitch:

> Tenable tells Detroit what is vulnerable. Detroit Cyber Ready tells Detroit when something changes that could matter right now, investigates it automatically, determines which city service could be affected, and tells the team what to do next.

**Language discipline:** never say "getting hacked in real time" or "under attack." External intelligence cannot prove that. Say "new external threat signal," "evidence of targeting," "potential exposure," "requires investigation." A judge or a CISO who catches an overclaim stops trusting everything else on the screen.

## 911 is the anchor, not one of twelve

The most important thing the CISO of the City of Detroit protects is 911. Everything in this product is built outward from that, and the design follows.

**911 is not a service in a list. It is the thing the list is measured against.**

Practical consequences, all of which are reflected in the sections below:

1. **A dedicated life-safety criticality tier** above `critical`. Any confirmed exposure reaching a life-safety service is P1 regardless of what the numeric score says. A CISO does not want a formula to be able to talk him out of paging someone about dispatch.
2. **911 stays one box on the screen.** No CAD, no PSAP, no ESInet, no ANI/ALI. The moment the demo shows a seven-box decomposition of 911, the audience is reading an architecture diagram instead of watching a city service turn amber. The internal detail may exist in notes and in the pitch answer if a judge digs, but it never renders.
3. **A dependency graph at exactly one level: city service depends on shared infrastructure.** 911 depends on Remote Access, Identity, and the Network Core. That is it. The realistic path to a dispatch outage is not a vulnerability in a dispatch product, it is a vulnerability in a boring shared component the city runs for everything.

Point 3 is the core insight and it is what Tenable structurally cannot do:

> Tenable can tell Detroit that a remote access appliance is vulnerable. It cannot tell Detroit that the appliance is one hop from 911.

**This shapes the demo.** The signal does not mention 911. It lands on shared Remote Access infrastructure, and the system draws a single line from there to 911. One hop, one line, two boxes. That reveal is stronger than a direct vendor match, it reflects how these incidents actually unfold, and it avoids implying that any named public safety vendor's product is compromised.

**Visual discipline, because this is where demos are lost:** twelve city services on the map, a thin infrastructure tier underneath, and never more than one dependency line drawn at a time. If the blast radius panel ever looks like a network diagram, it has failed.

**Resident impact is expressed in the only unit that matters here:** dispatch delay and response time. Not CVSS, not a risk score. "Potential disruption to emergency call handling, dispatch, and response times" is the sentence a mayor repeats.

**Pillar, settled.** Primary is Open, Accessible & Responsible Government, covering all twelve services. 911 is the anchor and the emotional center, and it is presented as the sharpest example of the city's responsibility to keep the services residents depend on running. Do not hedge between pillars on stage. Name one.

## Upstream integration seams

Verified against the existing repos. Nothing in `@casky/*` is published to npm and nothing is in an image registry, so "dependency" means submodule or HTTP.

### Seam A: the investigation agent, written here

**Resolved in Block 0: `casky-ai/claude-skills-security` is a PRIVATE repository.** `detroit-cyber-ready` must be public so judges can verify that every commit is dated this weekend, and a public repo carrying a private submodule hands outsiders a clone failure. So there is no platform submodule.

The consequence is that the investigation agent is written in this repo, in `packages/investigate/`. That turned out to be the better design regardless of the visibility question, for a reason worth stating:

**Casky's classifier is built for a different input.** `classifyEvidence()` takes free-text evidence, a wall of logs an analyst pasted, and its first job is to work out what happened. Detroit Cyber Ready never has that problem. Our input is already structured before the agent is invoked:

```ts
{ signal, landedOn, service, dependency, riskScore, riskComponents }
```

We know the CVE. We know which asset it landed on. We know which service it reaches and over which dependency. The deterministic layer has already answered "what happened" and "does it matter." The agent's job is narrower and different: explain the exposure in the city's terms, select investigation steps from the skills corpus, and write an ordered action plan with owners and SLAs.

Importing a free-text evidence classifier to do that would mean synthesizing fake evidence prose from structured data purely to feed a pipeline that would then try to re-derive the structure we already had. That is worse than writing the right agent.

What we keep from Casky is the **architecture**, which is the part that took the learning, not the code:
- `AbortController` passed in the options argument, never in params.
- `COMPLETE_MARKER` as the truth signal, checked independently at every layer.
- `Promise.allSettled` adapter fan-out where a failing source becomes a gap, never an exception.
- Prompt caching on stable system prompts.
- JSON repair on model output, because model JSON is unreliable by default.

Roughly 200 to 300 lines. It is on the Block 2 critical path and it is genuinely our work this weekend.

`@casky/ui-kit` is Apache-2.0 and would have been available, but is not used either. The UI is built fresh so the product reads as built for Detroit rather than as Casky with a new logo.

### Seam B: Casky hosted API over HTTPS

Auth is `Authorization: Bearer csk_<key>`, scopes `plans:read` and `runs:write`. The key must be minted once through the logged-in Casky web UI (there is no API-key route to mint an API key) and stored as `CASKY_API_KEY`.

| Endpoint | Use |
|---|---|
| `GET https://casky.ai/api/v1/playbooks?techniques=T1190,T1133` | Pull matching investigation playbooks for the validated techniques |
| `GET https://casky.ai/api/v1/cve-spotlights?cve_ids=CVE-2025-4427` | Pull Casky's existing CVE analysis, CVSS, KEV flag, technique mapping, skill mapping |

Both are behind a `caskyApiAdapter` implementing `ContextEngineAdapter`, so a Casky outage degrades to an evidence gap instead of failing the investigation. That degradation is itself a demo asset: it proves the architecture.

### Seam C: skills corpus

The `upstream/skills` submodule ships `index.json` with 817 skills carrying `subdomain`, `tags`, `mitre_attack` and `nist_csf` frontmatter. We read it off disk at build time, filter to the ~60 skills relevant to municipal infrastructure, and pass that narrowed list to `classifyEvidence` as `availableSkills`. Offline safe, no API call, no Supabase.

### Explicitly out of scope for the weekend

`casky-runner` (Python, Docker, GHCR) is named in the architecture diagram as the execution tier and demoed only if there is spare time on Sunday. It has no git tags, no `pyproject.toml`, and requires a Docker socket plus the skills-library image. That is too much integration risk for a 54 hour window. The slide says "execution tier, available today via Casky Box," which is true.

## Architecture

```
  THREAT SIGNALS (real, live)          SURFACE SIGNALS (synthetic snapshots)
  CISA KEV · NVD · EPSS ·              seeded surface observations,
  CISA Advisories · abuse.ch ·         diffed T0 -> T1
  GreyNoise                            (Shodan/Censys shape, no scanning)
            |                                      |
            +------------------+-------------------+
                              |
                              v
  packages/signals      normalize -> fingerprint -> dedupe -> store
                              |
                              v
  packages/impact       STAGE 1: COMPONENT MATCH (deterministic, no LLM)
                        signal vendor/product/CPE  x  technology inventory
                              |
                        match? --no--> record "no Detroit exposure", show it, stop
                              |
                             yes
                              v
  packages/impact       STAGE 2: DEPENDENCY PROPAGATION  <-- the differentiated part
                        walk service_dependencies outward, max 3 hops
                        "this VPN box is 1 hop from dispatch"
                              |
                              v
  UPSTREAM: Casky       assembleContextGraph([ threatIntelAdapter,
  agent harness                              detroitAssetAdapter,
                                             caskyApiAdapter ])
                              |
                        classifyEvidence()
                        validate techniques -> select skills ->
                        order steps -> identify evidence gaps
                              |
                              v
  packages/impact       RISK + RESIDENT IMPACT SCORE (explainable, 4 components)
                              |
                              v
  apps/web              ACTION PLAN -> CISO ALERT -> INCIDENT TICKET
                        streamed live over SSE

  FUTURE CONNECTORS (architecture slide only, not built)
  Tenable · SIEM · EDR · CMDB · Identity · Firewall  -->  feed the inventory
```

The one idea that makes this defensible: **the match is deterministic, the narrative is generated.** A judge or a CISO can audit exactly why a signal fired. The LLM never decides whether Detroit is exposed. It explains, prioritizes and writes, which is what it is good at.

## Data model

Synthetic inventory, clearly labeled. Every screen carries a `Simulated Inventory` watermark. Threat feeds are 100 percent real and live.

```sql
create table city_services (
  slug              text primary key,       -- '911-emergency-communications'
  name              text not null,
  department        text not null,          -- 'Detroit Public Safety / ITS'
  -- 'life-safety' is its own tier above 'critical'. 911 is the only member at launch.
  criticality       text not null check (criticality in ('life-safety','critical','high','moderate','low')),
  resident_impact   text not null,          -- plain English, shown to the CISO verbatim
  impact_unit       text,                   -- for life-safety: 'dispatch delay and response time'
  externally_exposed boolean not null default false,
  lat               numeric, lon numeric    -- city map placement
);

-- The connective tissue. City-wide, shared across many services, NOT drawn on the map.
-- Deliberately small: four or five entries, never more. This is the whole second tier.
create table shared_infrastructure (
  slug        text primary key,             -- 'remote-access' | 'identity' | 'network-core'
                                            -- | 'records-management' | 'payment-gateway'
  name        text not null,                -- 'Remote Access', shown to humans
  description text not null,                -- one line, plain English
  exposure    text not null check (exposure in ('internet-facing','partner-network','internal'))
);

-- Exactly one level: a city service depends on shared infrastructure.
-- No infrastructure-to-infrastructure edges. No service-to-service edges.
-- This constraint is what keeps the blast radius panel readable.
create table service_dependencies (
  id              uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  service_slug    text not null references city_services(slug),
  infrastructure_slug text not null references shared_infrastructure(slug),
  kind            text not null,            -- 'reachable-via' | 'authenticates-via'
                                            -- | 'routes-over' | 'reads-from'
  criticality     text not null check (criticality in ('hard','soft')),
  -- hard: the service is unavailable if this fails. soft: degraded.
  rationale       text not null,            -- ONE sentence, shown verbatim on stage
  unique (service_slug, infrastructure_slug)
);

-- Technology attaches to a service OR to shared infrastructure, never both.
create table technologies (
  id          uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  service_slug        text references city_services(slug),
  infrastructure_slug text references shared_infrastructure(slug),
  check (num_nonnulls(service_slug, infrastructure_slug) = 1),
  vendor      text not null,                -- matched against KEV vendorProject
  product     text not null,                -- matched against KEV product
  version     text,
  cpe         text,                         -- optional, preferred match key when present
  exposure    text not null check (exposure in ('internet-facing','partner-network','internal'))
);

create table signals (
  id           uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  source       text not null,               -- 'cisa-kev' | 'nvd' | 'epss' | 'cisa-advisory' | 'abusech' | 'greynoise' | 'surface'
  provenance   text not null check (provenance in ('live','synthetic')),
  external_id  text not null,               -- 'CVE-2025-4427'
  fingerprint  text not null unique,        -- sha256(source|external_id|material_fields)
  kind         text not null,               -- 'kev-addition' | 'advisory' | 'ioc' | 'scanning-activity' | 'surface-change'
  title        text not null,
  summary      text,
  published_at timestamptz not null,
  severity     text,
  raw          jsonb not null,
  first_seen_at timestamptz not null default now()
);

-- Point-in-time externally visible surface. Populated by any SurfaceSource.
-- Synthetic this weekend, Shodan or Censys when productized. Same shape either way.
create table surface_observations (
  id            uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  snapshot_id   uuid not null,              -- groups one complete point-in-time view
  source_name   text not null,              -- 'synthetic' | 'shodan' | 'censys'
  provenance    text not null check (provenance in ('live','synthetic')),
  host          text not null,
  ip            inet not null,
  port          int not null,
  transport     text not null,
  service       text not null,
  vendor        text not null,
  product       text not null,
  version       text,
  cpe           text,
  tls_cert_sha256 text,
  tls_not_after timestamptz,
  banner        text,
  service_slug  text references city_services(slug),   -- which service this host belongs to
  observed_at   timestamptz not null
);
create index on surface_observations (snapshot_id);
create unique index on surface_observations (snapshot_id, host, port, transport);

create table signal_matches (
  id             uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  signal_id      uuid not null references signals(id),
  -- where the signal actually landed
  landed_on      text not null,             -- slug of a service OR of shared infrastructure
  landed_kind    text not null check (landed_kind in ('service','infrastructure')),
  -- which city service it reaches
  service_slug   text not null references city_services(slug),
  match_basis    text not null,             -- 'cpe' | 'vendor+product' | 'advisory-keyword' | 'surface-host'
  matched_on     jsonb not null,            -- exactly what matched, for the audit trail
  -- 0 = landed directly on this service's own technology.
  -- 1 = landed on shared infrastructure this service depends on. There is no 2.
  hops           int not null default 0,
  dependency     jsonb,                     -- {infrastructure, kind, criticality, rationale}
  provenance     text not null check (provenance in ('live','synthetic')),
  confidence     numeric not null,
  unique (signal_id, landed_on, service_slug)
);

create table investigations (
  id            uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  signal_id     uuid not null references signals(id),
  service_slug  text not null references city_services(slug),
  status        text not null default 'queued',  -- queued|running|completed|failed
  context_graph jsonb,
  plan          jsonb,
  risk_score    numeric,
  risk_components jsonb,                     -- the four components, for the UI bars
  output        text,                        -- carries COMPLETE_MARKER on success
  started_at    timestamptz,
  ended_at      timestamptz
);

create table actions (
  id               uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  investigation_id uuid not null references investigations(id),
  rank             int not null,
  title            text not null,
  detail           text not null,
  sla              text not null,            -- 'Now' | '< 1 hour' | '< 24 hours' | 'Ongoing'
  owner            text not null
);
```

`signal_matches.matched_on` is the audit trail. When the CISO asks "why did you flag 911," the answer is a JSON row, not a model output.

## Signal sources

Six adapters, one interface. Only the first three are load bearing for the demo.

```ts
export interface SignalSource {
  readonly name: string;
  readonly requiresKey: boolean;
  /** Feeds that fail return [] and push a gap. Never throw. */
  poll(since: Date): Promise<{ signals: RawSignal[]; gaps: string[] }>;
}
```

| Adapter | Endpoint | Key | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cisa-kev` | `cisa.gov/sites/default/files/feeds/known_exploited_vulnerabilities.json` | none | **Primary trigger.** Carries `vendorProject`, `product`, `dateAdded`, `requiredAction`, `dueDate`. The `vendorProject`/`product` fields are what make the deterministic match possible. |
| `nvd` | `services.nvd.nist.gov/rest/json/cves/2.0` | optional | CVSS, CWE, CPE configurations. CPE gives the higher confidence match path. |
| `epss` | `api.first.org/data/v1/epss` | none | Exploitation probability and percentile. Feeds the risk score. |
| `cisa-advisories` | CISA advisories and ICS advisories RSS | none | Campaign level context, keyword matched against the inventory. |
| `abusech` | URLhaus / ThreatFox | free key | Malicious infrastructure IOCs. Corroborating signal count only. |
| `greynoise` | Community API | free key | Mass scanning versus targeted. Corroborating signal count only. |

Shodan and Censys are **not called**. With a synthetic inventory there is nothing legitimate to look up, and running real recon against City of Detroit infrastructure without written authorization is not something we do. Their capability is replaced by a synthetic `SurfaceSource` that emits the same record shape, so the diff engine we build this weekend is the one that ships. See the next section.

Reliability rules, copied from the pattern in `claude-skills-security/apps/web/app/api/cron/cve-spotlight-daily/route.ts`:

- Every fetch gets `AbortSignal.timeout(...)`, 15s for NVD, 8s for everything else.
- Every fetch is individually try/caught. One dead feed is an evidence gap, never a failed poll.
- Every signal is fingerprinted and upserted, so re-polling is idempotent.
- Every successful poll writes a snapshot to `data/detroit/snapshots/`. If `DEMO_MODE=1` or a fetch fails, the snapshot is served. **We never live-fetch during the pitch.**

## Synthetic data strategy and productization hooks

The constraint: no live scanning, no real target systems, everything on the asset side is synthetic. The design rule that makes this a strength rather than a limitation:

> **Threat intelligence is real. Detroit's geography and service catalog are real, from the city's own open data. Only the technology inventory is synthetic. Every synthetic source implements the exact interface its real replacement will implement, and both must pass the same contract test suite.**

This is not a hackathon shortcut dressed up. It is the correct product posture. Casky does not scan cities. A city hands over its inventory, or we ingest it from the Tenable, CMDB and CrowdStrike deployments it already paid for. The synthetic inventory is a stand-in for a customer-supplied inventory, which is what the real thing will always be.

### Real Detroit data, from data.detroitmi.gov

This is a significant upgrade to credibility and it shrinks the synthetic surface to one layer. The City of Detroit publishes an open data portal with real geography and real facility data. We use it for everything that is not a security claim:

| Layer | Source | Real or synthetic |
|---|---|---|
| City boundary, neighborhoods, council districts | data.detroitmi.gov | **Real** |
| Facility locations: police precincts, fire and EMS stations, libraries, parks, rec centers, DDOT routes and stops | data.detroitmi.gov | **Real** |
| Department names and service catalog | data.detroitmi.gov | **Real** |
| Threat intelligence: KEV, NVD, EPSS, advisories | CISA, NIST, FIRST | **Real** |
| Which vendor and product each service runs | authored | **Synthetic, labeled** |
| The dependency edges between services and infrastructure | authored | **Synthetic, labeled** |
| External attack surface observations | `SyntheticSurfaceSource` | **Synthetic, labeled** |

The map is then a real map of Detroit with real facilities on it, which is worth a great deal visually and costs nothing in risk, because a library's location is public information and carries no security implication.

**Implementation:** the portal is an ArcGIS Hub instance, so datasets expose GeoJSON and REST endpoints. Pull once during Block 1, commit the GeoJSON to `data/detroit/geo/`, and read it from disk at build time. Do not fetch it at request time and do not fetch it during the demo.

**Fallback, and this is not optional.** Exact dataset identifiers must be verified in Block 1 and portals change without notice. Write the extraction as a one-off script whose output is committed, so a portal outage on Sunday is irrelevant. If a dataset cannot be found in 30 minutes, fall back to hand-placed coordinates for the twelve services and move on. The map is polish, the pipeline is the product. Do not spend Saturday on GeoJSON.

### Three connector contracts

Everything that crosses the boundary into this system implements one of three interfaces. Nothing else is allowed to reach out to the network.

**1. `SignalSource`, the threat side. Live today.**

```ts
export interface SignalSource {
  readonly name: string;
  readonly mode: 'live' | 'synthetic';
  readonly requiresKey: boolean;
  /** Never throws. A failure returns [] plus a gap string. */
  poll(since: Date): Promise<{ signals: RawSignal[]; gaps: string[] }>;
}
```

**2. `SurfaceSource`, the exposure side. Synthetic today, Shodan or Censys tomorrow.**

This is the Shodan and Censys replacement. We model their *output*, which is a stable, well documented shape, rather than their API.

```ts
export interface SurfaceObservation {
  host: string;              // 'svc-cad-01.detroit.example'  (RFC 2606 reserved domain)
  ip: string;                // RFC 5737 documentation range, 198.51.100.x
  port: number;
  transport: 'tcp' | 'udp';
  service: string;           // 'https'
  vendor: string;            // matched against service_technologies.vendor
  product: string;           // matched against service_technologies.product
  version: string | null;
  cpe: string | null;
  tls_cert_sha256: string | null;
  tls_not_after: string | null;
  banner: string | null;
  observed_at: string;       // ISO 8601
}

export interface SurfaceSource {
  readonly name: string;
  readonly mode: 'live' | 'synthetic';
  /** One complete point-in-time view of the externally visible surface. */
  snapshot(scope: SurfaceScope): Promise<{ observations: SurfaceObservation[]; gaps: string[] }>;
}
```

`SyntheticSurfaceSource` reads committed snapshot files from `data/detroit/surface/`. `ShodanSurfaceSource` and `CensysSurfaceSource` are stub files committed this weekend containing the class shell, the config keys they will need, and a `throw new Error('not enabled')`. They exist so the wiring point is visible in the repo, not just on a slide.

Hosts and IPs use RFC 2606 reserved domains (`.example`) and RFC 5737 documentation IP ranges. Nothing in the seed data resolves to a real system, deliberately and verifiably.

**3. `ContextEngineAdapter`, the enrichment side.** Already defined upstream in `@casky/investigate/context-engines/adapter`. Tenable, Wiz, CrowdStrike, ServiceNow and Microsoft all land here when productized. We implement three this weekend (`threatIntelAdapter`, `detroitAssetAdapter`, `caskyApiAdapter`) against the same interface those will use.

### The surface diff engine, real code on synthetic input

```ts
// packages/signals/src/surface-diff.ts
export function diffSurface(
  before: SurfaceObservation[],
  after: SurfaceObservation[]
): SurfaceChange[]
```

Change kinds detected: `new-service`, `removed-service`, `version-changed`, `certificate-changed`, `certificate-expiring`, `new-host`. Each becomes a `RawSignal` with `kind: 'surface-change'` and flows into the identical pipeline as a KEV addition: match, investigate, score, act.

This is the part worth saying out loud to judges. **Two completely different signal types, one pipeline, zero branching in the investigation layer.** That is the Solution criterion (elegant, repeatable, scalable) demonstrated rather than asserted.

### Provenance stamping

Every signal, observation and match row carries `provenance` (`'live'` or `'synthetic'`) and `source_name`. The UI renders a `Simulated` chip on any card whose provenance is synthetic, and the CISO alert footer lists which inputs were live and which were simulated.

Three reasons this is non-negotiable:
1. Honesty on stage. A judge can see at a glance what is real (the CISA KEV entry) and what is not (the 911 server).
2. It is how the real product will work anyway. A city onboarding in week one has live threat feeds and a partially populated inventory. Mixed mode is the steady state, not a transition.
3. It makes the swap to live data a configuration change with a visible, auditable effect rather than a silent one.

## The exposure match

The heart of the product. Deterministic, no LLM, two stages.

```ts
// packages/impact/src/exposure.ts

/** Stage 1: what did this signal land on, a service or shared infrastructure? */
export function matchSignalToAssets(
  signal: RawSignal,
  inventory: Technology[]
): AssetMatch[]

/** Stage 2: if it landed on infrastructure, which city services depend on it? */
export function propagateToServices(
  matches: AssetMatch[],
  dependencies: ServiceDependency[]
): SignalMatch[]
```

### Stage 1: asset match

Highest confidence first.

1. **CPE match** (confidence 0.95). NVD `configurations` CPE against `service_technologies.cpe`. Version range aware.
2. **Vendor plus product match** (confidence 0.80). KEV `vendorProject` and `product` normalized (lowercase, strip punctuation, strip corporate suffixes) against inventory vendor and product.
3. **Surface host match** (confidence 0.85). A `surface-change` signal carries a host that maps directly to a service or an infrastructure entry.
4. **Advisory keyword match** (confidence 0.45). Advisory title and description against vendor and product names. Marked low confidence in the UI, never alone sufficient to raise a P1.

### Stage 2: dependency propagation, the part that matters

**One hop. Not a graph walk, a single join.** If the signal landed on a city service's own technology, `hops = 0` and we are done. If it landed on shared infrastructure, every service with a `service_dependencies` row pointing at that infrastructure is affected at `hops = 1`, with confidence multiplied by 0.85.

There is deliberately no `MAX_HOPS`, no breadth-first search, and no transitive closure. A second hop would be both harder to defend to a CISO and impossible to draw legibly on a slide. If the model ever needs more depth, that is a post-hackathon problem.

Worked example, which is also the demo:

```
CISA KEV addition: remote access appliance, actively exploited
  -> stage 1: lands on infrastructure 'remote-access'   (vendor+product, 0.80)
  -> stage 2: 911-emergency-communications --[reachable-via, hard]--> remote-access
             1 hop, confidence 0.80 * 0.85 = 0.68
  -> service is life-safety tier, dependency is hard
  -> P1 escalation rule fires
```

The blast radius panel renders two boxes and one line, and reads the authored rationale verbatim:

> **Remote Access** ──hard──> **911 Emergency Communications**
>
> Dispatch reaches its systems through the remote access tier. If remote access is unavailable or compromised, dispatch is affected.

That sentence is the product. It is the thing the CISO cannot get from the tools he already owns. It is also, notably, one sentence a non-technical judge understands on first hearing.

Stage 2 also finds the *other* services that depend on Remote Access. Show the count ("3 other services affected") but do not draw their lines during the demo. 911 gets the line.

### Non-matches are shown

No match means the signal is recorded as `no Detroit exposure` and displayed in the feed as informational, with the reason. **Showing the signals that did not match is important.** It proves the system is filtering rather than alarming, which is the exact complaint the CISO had about his current tools. A feed that only ever shows red is the thing he is trying to get away from.

## Risk and resident impact scoring

Explainable, four components, rendered as four bars in the UI so a human can audit it.

```
risk = 40 * kev_active            // 1 if in CISA KEV, else 0
     + 25 * epss_percentile       // 0 to 1, from FIRST.org
     + 20 * criticality_weight    // life-safety 1.0, critical 0.8, high 0.6,
                                  //   moderate 0.4, low 0.2
     + 15 * reach_confidence       // stage 2 output: match confidence after hop decay

// worked example, the demo scenario:
// KEV addition on a remote access appliance, EPSS p85,
// reaching 911 (life-safety) at 1 hop over a hard dependency, confidence 0.68
// 40*1 + 25*0.85 + 20*1.0 + 15*0.68 = 40 + 21.25 + 20 + 10.2 = 91.45  -> 91 / 100
```

Priority mapping: `>= 80` P1, `60 to 79` P2, `40 to 59` P3, `< 40` informational.

### The life-safety escalation rule

The formula can be overridden upward, never downward:

```ts
// A score is allowed to raise priority. It is never allowed to lower it
// below what the life-safety rule demands.
if (service.criticality === 'life-safety' && signal.kev_active && path.every(e => e.criticality === 'hard')) {
  priority = 'P1';
  escalation_reason = 'Life-safety service reachable over a hard dependency from actively exploited shared infrastructure.';
}
```

This exists because a formula that can talk a CISO out of paging someone about dispatch is a formula that will eventually do exactly that. The rule is shown in the UI whenever it fires, with its reason, so the override is visible rather than mysterious. Hard-coding a stated policy is the correct behavior here, and it is worth saying so if a judge asks why there is an `if` statement in the middle of a scoring model.

Resident impact is **not** computed. It is authored per service in `services.yaml` and shown verbatim, because a sentence a human wrote is more trustworthy and more quotable than a sentence a model wrote:

```yaml
- slug: 911-emergency-communications
  name: 911 Emergency Communications
  department: Detroit Public Safety / ITS
  criticality: life-safety
  impact_unit: dispatch delay and response time
  resident_impact: >
    Potential disruption to emergency call handling, dispatch, and response times.
  depends_on:
    - infrastructure: remote-access
      kind: reachable-via
      criticality: hard
      rationale: >
        Dispatch reaches its systems through the remote access tier. If remote
        access is unavailable or compromised, dispatch is affected.
    - infrastructure: identity
      kind: authenticates-via
      criticality: hard
      rationale: >
        Call takers sign in through city identity.
    - infrastructure: network-core
      kind: routes-over
      criticality: hard
      rationale: >
        Emergency call delivery routes over the city network core.
    - infrastructure: records-management
      kind: reads-from
      criticality: soft
      rationale: >
        Dispatch pulls incident history from records management. Loss degrades
        context, it does not stop dispatch.
```

Three notes on this shape.

**No components.** 911 is one entry. The internal detail (CAD, PSAP, ESInet, ANI/ALI) lives in the pitch answer if a judge asks how deep the model goes, and nowhere on screen.

**Each `rationale` is one sentence, written to be read aloud.** These get spoken during the demo. Author them as speech, not documentation.

**The `soft` edge on records management is deliberate.** It gives the demo something to correctly *not* escalate, which is how you prove the system filters rather than alarms. If every dependency is hard, the model is just a list.

## The five layer guard discipline

Carried over from the Casky engineering standard, because a demo that hangs on stage is a lost hackathon. An investigation must reach a terminal state through at least three independent paths.

| Layer | Responsibility | Guard |
|---|---|---|
| L1 Anthropic API | Token generation and stream termination | `AbortController` hard wall, signal passed in the **options** argument, not params |
| L2 Investigation writer | Content assembly and atomic status write | Single `UPDATE` carrying `status='completed'`, `output` with `COMPLETE_MARKER`, and `ended_at` |
| L3 Cron heal | Rescue orphaned investigations | Detect the marker before ever writing `failed` |
| L4 SSE route | Stream to the client | Poll for the marker, not just the `status` column |
| L5 UI | Last resort escape hatch | Marker detection in streamed text plus a stale banner below the SSE timeout |

`apps/web/lib/constants.ts` holds `COMPLETE_MARKER` and is imported by all five layers. It never gets hardcoded anywhere.

SSE contract, matching the shape already proven in `claude-skills-security/apps/web/app/api/runs/[id]/stream/route.ts`:

- Poll interval 2000ms, max duration 270000ms (under the Vercel 300s function limit).
- Unnamed `data: {"text": "..."}` frames for output deltas, streamed by byte offset into `output`.
- Named events: `step` (investigation step state change, which drives the panel 3 checklist), `done`, `timeout`.
- A `healIfNeeded()` that completes a run whose marker landed but whose status write was lost.
